Recommendation

Launch on pay as you go as the primary model: list each token type at the market median, win a discount from the partner to set cost, and give the customer a discount off the median up to the point margin stays healthy. Keep the subscription as an optional plan for known high-volume accounts. One usage rate anchored to the median is simple to sell and sits Akamai squarely in the middle of the market.

The goal is adoption. Margin is a floor to protect, not a number to maximise; revenue follows from developers, token growth and retention.

Three key reasons

1. Willingness to pay and market standing

Akamai resells a white-labelled model with the same performance as the partner. Without a hard inference-path differentiator (edge security, WAF, prompt-injection defence, PII handling), buyers will not pay a premium over the field. Listing at the market median places Akamai at the middle of the market by construction, and the customer discount moves it below the median where the sale needs it.

2. Revenue and margin hold together

Cost is the partner retail less the partner discount; the list is the median less the customer discount. At the current customer discount of the blended gross margin is . A deeper partner discount widens the room to discount the customer without eroding margin, so the negotiation with the partner directly funds adoption.

3. Brand, trust and simplicity

A single all-in price anchored to the market median reads as simple and fair, with no fee line to defend. The optional cap keeps the list from ever exceeding the partner retail or the median, whichever is chosen. The durable edge is enterprise trust, an integrated cloud with data locality, one bill and no egress, and a global day-one launch. These earn the customer's choice, not a per-token premium.

Why the subscription is a second model, not the default

A subscription only pays off at high, sustained usage, because the effective price is the fee divided by tokens used. Light users overpay and churn; heavy users can outrun a filled free bundle and turn the account into a loss, as the subscription sensitivity grid shows. It is worth offering to a known high-volume segment or as a metered plan, but pay as you go scales cleanly across every cohort and is the right default.

Model catalog — market median by token type, outliers removed

For each model, the market median of the input, cached, and output price is taken across every provider on OpenRouter, after removing statistical outliers. An outlier is a price outside the interquartile fence for that token type, below Q1 − 1.5×IQR or above Q3 + 1.5×IQR; the clean median is the median of the prices that remain. The raw median across all providers is shown beside each clean figure for reference. The outlier-excluded median is a more defensible basis for Model 1's market-median ceiling than the raw median; the pricing tabs currently read the raw median.

Blended column uses the current token mix (, input/cached/output). Source: OpenRouter endpoint data retrieved 20 Aug 2026.

Outliers removed

Providers whose price fell outside the interquartile fence for a token type and were excluded before the median was taken. A robust median is little moved by outliers, so the clean and raw figures often match; the exclusions matter most where a few providers price far from the pack.
